Many task apps begin by asking you to organize your life. Create a project, choose a date, set a priority, add a reminder, sort a list. Those features can be useful later. They are a lot to ask of someone who is having trouble starting one task right now.

Kindling is built around a much smaller sequence: name the task, choose one tiny step, and start a two-minute timer.

## Make the first action smaller than the plan

"Clean the apartment" is not a first action. "Put one cup in the sink" is. The second sentence gives your hands something to do without requiring you to believe you can finish the entire job.

The interface should help someone find that first motion and then get out of the way. A large task can remain large; Kindling does not need to pretend it has become easy. It needs to make beginning less abstract.

This is particularly relevant to the ADHD use case Kindling is designed for. I do not want the app to speak as though a timer is treatment or a universal solution. People have different needs, and the friction around starting can have many causes. The product offers one practical tool for one moment.

## Two minutes is a boundary, not a promise

A short timer lowers the size of the commitment. The user can stop when it ends. They can also continue if the first action gave them enough momentum. Either outcome is valid.

That distinction matters in the copy and the visual design. A streak, a score, or an elaborate completion ceremony could make a small attempt feel like a test. Kindling should acknowledge the start without turning it into another obligation.

## The honest scope of a small app

Kindling does not need to become a calendar, team workspace, or productivity dashboard to justify itself. Its job is to help a person cross the gap between knowing what they want to do and touching the first piece of it.

The app was marked in review when I listed it on this portfolio. I am writing about its design here, not claiming an outcome from a release or inventing a set of user results. The question I want to keep testing is simple: when the task feels too big, does the app help make the next action clear enough to begin?
